WHAT THE DIAGNOSIS MEANS
Record IECTC10_0002
Measured gases (ppm): H2 37800, CH4 1740, C2H6 249, C2H4 8, C2H2 8. These values come from the data file as they are.

Gas level: at least one gas is above the typical value of IEC 60599 (Annex A), so the classical methods look at the gas ratios to name a fault type.

Classical methods:
- Rogers ratio: ND, no diagnosis: the ratios fall in no coded combination.
- IEC 60599 ratio: PD, partial discharges.
- Duval Triangle 1: PD, partial discharges.

Machine learning (Random Forest, model 1.0): most probable type PD (partial discharges), probability 81 %.
The next most probable types are D1 (12 %) and T1 (5 %).
The model has a clear majority for one type (more than half of the probability).

Agreement (amber lamp): 2 of the 3 classical methods agree with the ML answer. When methods disagree, the record is close to a boundary between fault types or is unusual: do not rely on one method alone.
Who differs from ML (PD): Rogers says ND. D1/D2 counts as agreeing with D1 or D2; DT and ND never count as agreeing.

HOW MUCH TO TRUST IT
This is decision support, not a verdict. On the inspection-confirmed IEC TC 10 cases used in this study, the ML model was not shown to be better than the classical methods (thesis, Chapter 4); use it as a second opinion. Confirm any fault indication with a repeat sample, the gas trend over time and, if needed, an inspection.

WHAT THE SIMULATION SHOWS
The transformer was simulated for 24 hours with an ambient temperature of 20 degC.
Top-oil temperature (blue): ends at 78.6 degC. Hot-spot temperature (red, the hottest point of the winding): peaks at 100.3 degC at 24.0 h and ends at 100.3 degC.
The load factor was constant at 0.98 times the rated load.
Insulation ageing: the paper insulation ages faster when the hot-spot is hotter. At a hot-spot of 98 degC the ageing rate is 1 (normal); every 6 K more doubles it (reference values of params_transformer.m, TO VERIFY against IEC 60076-7). Over this run the insulation aged as much as it would in 27.9 hours at the normal rate (loss of life).
The hot-spot was above 98 degC for part of the run, so the insulation aged faster than the normal rate during that time.
Remember: this is a thermal model of the operating condition. It gives context for the gas results; it does not prove or disprove a fault.
