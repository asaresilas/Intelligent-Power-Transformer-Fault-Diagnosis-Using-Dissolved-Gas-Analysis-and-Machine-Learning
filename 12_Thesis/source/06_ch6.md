# Statistical Comparison and Disagreement Analysis {#sec:ch6}

## Introduction

This chapter answers the first two research questions: how the accuracy of the machine-learning model compares with that of the conventional methods on the same records, and where and why the two disagree. It presents the main comparison with confidence intervals (@sec:s-main), the paired significance tests (@sec:s-tests), the sensitivity analyses (@sec:s-sens), the agreement between the methods (@sec:s-kappa) and the boundary analysis (@sec:s-boundary), and then judges the research hypothesis against the three criteria fixed in advance (@sec:s-verdict). The chapter ends with a discussion of the findings against the literature.

- **Internal test set.** The ML model was significantly more accurate than all three methods after Holm adjustment. Against the Duval Triangle it was right on {{mc_int_duv_b}} records on which Duval was wrong, against {{mc_int_duv_c}} the other way (adjusted p = {{mc_int_duv_pholm}}).
- **Li (2019) set.** The ML model was again significantly more accurate than all three methods; against Duval the adjusted p-value was {{mc_li_duv_pholm}}.
- **IEC TC 10 benchmark.** The ML model was significantly more accurate than the Rogers method (b = {{mc_iec_rog_b}}, c = {{mc_iec_rog_c}}, adjusted p = {{mc_iec_rog_pholm}}), but not than IEC 60599 (adjusted p = {{mc_iec_iecm_pholm}}). ## Sensitivity Analyses {#sec:s-sens}

Two sensitivity analyses test whether these conclusions depend on choices made in the main analysis.

**Normality gate.** Without the gate (@tab:sensitivity in Chapter 5) the conventional methods do better on the IEC TC 10 benchmark, because the gate declares some inspected faults Normal. The Duval Triangle then reaches a strict accuracy of {{pm_iec_no_gate_duv_strict}}, higher than the ML model's {{iec_ml_strict}}. The conclusion on the benchmark, that ML is not more accurate than the best conventional method, therefore holds even more strongly without the gate. On the internal test set, which contains Normal records, removing the gate makes the conventional methods worse.

**Full test sets.** @tab:allrecords repeats the comparison on the full test sets, including the records that copy benchmark cases and, for IEC TC 10, all equipment types. ## Agreement between the Methods {#sec:s-kappa}

@tab:kappa and @fig:kappa give Cohen's kappa between every pair of methods and between each method and the true class.

[[TABLE:kappa]] {#tab:kappa}

![Cohen's kappa between the true class and the four methods on the three primary test sets (1 = perfect agreement, 0 = agreement expected by chance)](12_Thesis/figures/thesis/kappa.png){#fig:kappa width=15.5}

Three patterns appear:

- **Agreement with the truth.** The ML model agrees best with the true class on the internal and Li sets (kappa {{k_int_true_ml}} and {{k_li_true_ml}}), but on the IEC TC 10 benchmark the Duval Triangle agrees better ({{k_iec_true_duv}}) than the ML model ({{k_iec_true_ml}}).
- **Agreement among the ratio methods.** The Rogers and IEC 60599 methods agree strongly with each other (kappa {{k_int_rog_iecm}} on the internal test set), as expected from two methods that use the same three ratios.
- **Agreement between ML and the conventional methods.** The ML model agrees only fairly to moderately with each conventional method (for example {{k_int_ml_rog}} with Rogers and {{k_int_ml_iecm}} with IEC 60599 on the internal test set). The ML model and the conventional methods therefore reach their answers in different ways, and showing both gives the engineer information that neither gives alone.

Criterion 2 cannot hold for the Duval Triangle, which always gives an answer, so that its strict and conditional accuracy are equal. Criterion 3 is judged on the pooled records with the normality gate (@tab:boundary).

The research hypothesis is therefore **not supported** on the main benchmark. On the inspection-confirmed IEC TC 10 power transformers the ML model is not significantly more accurate than the IEC 60599 method or the Duval Triangle, its advantage over the ratio methods does not come only from the cases they cannot diagnose, and disagreement is not concentrated near the decision boundaries in the main analysis. On the internal and Li sets the hypothesis is partly supported: there the ML model is significantly more accurate than every conventional method.

## Discussion

### Why the Benchmark Gives a Different Picture

The strong performance of the Duval Triangle on the IEC TC 10 benchmark contrasts with studies that report very low Duval accuracy on pooled data, such as 35.8 % (Dladla and Thango, 2025b), and with the large ML advantages reported elsewhere (Al-Sakini *et al.*, 2024; Dladla and Thango, 2025b). Two explanations are likely. First, the IEC TC 10 cases are the kind of clear, inspected faults on which the conventional methods were built, and the IEC 60599 limits were refined on these very cases (Duval and dePablo, 2001); the conventional methods therefore enjoy a home advantage. Second, scoring on five merged classes removes the finest thermal distinctions, where the triangle is weakest. On the internal and Li sets, whose labels are of unknown origin, the conventional methods did much worse and the ML model was significantly better than all of them. Which of these pictures is closer to field practice can be decided only with new inspection-confirmed data.

### Why ML and the Conventional Methods Disagree

The kappa values show that the ML model and the conventional methods often disagree. The boundary analysis shows that, for the Duval Triangle, much of this disagreement arises near zone boundaries when the raw answers are compared; this agrees with the view that the fixed boundaries of the conventional methods are a main source of their uncertainty (Zhang *et al.*, 2022; Kashiwagi *et al.*, 2025). In the main analysis the normality gate dominates: it declared {{conv_normal_int}} of the 61 internal-test records and {{conv_normal_li}} of the 86 Li records Normal, most of them faulty records, and on a faulty record the ML model, which assigns a fault class, disagrees with such a Normal answer whatever the position of the record relative to the boundaries. For the ratio methods, a large share of the disagreements are no-diagnosis answers, which by definition do not lie at a particular boundary.

### Implications

The comparison does not show that ML should replace the conventional methods. On the confirmed faults of the main benchmark the best conventional method was at least as accurate. It does show three things in favour of using ML alongside them: the ML model always gives an answer; on the two other sources it was significantly more accurate than every method; and its errors are partly different from theirs. Showing both diagnoses side by side, and treating disagreement as a signal for closer attention, is the use that the evidence supports. Chapter 8 shows how this is done in the integrated framework.

## Summary

On the internal and Li test sets the ML model was significantly more accurate than every conventional method. On the inspection-confirmed IEC TC 10 benchmark it was significantly more accurate than the Rogers method only, did not differ significantly from IEC 60599, and was behind the Duval Triangle in point estimate; without the normality gate the Duval Triangle was clearly ahead. The ML model and the conventional methods agreed only fairly to moderately. Disagreement with the Duval Triangle concentrated near its zone boundaries when the raw answers were compared, but not in the main analysis with the gate. The research hypothesis is therefore not supported on the main benchmark and partly supported on the other two sets.
