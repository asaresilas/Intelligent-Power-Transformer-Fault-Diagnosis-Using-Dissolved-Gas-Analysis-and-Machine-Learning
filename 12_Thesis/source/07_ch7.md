# Diagnosis with Fewer Gases and Model Improvement {#sec:ch7}

## Introduction

This chapter reports two further analyses. The first answers the third research question: how much diagnostic performance is lost when fewer gases are measured, as happens with many online monitors, and what is the smallest useful gas panel (@sec:g-results). The second is the exploratory study, carried out after the main results were known, of whether the model can be improved by pooling the data sources, by adding the conventional diagnoses as inputs, or by letting the model abstain when it is unsure (@sec:g-study2).

- The best three-gas panel (C2H6, C2H4 and C2H2) lost {{rg_best3_loss}}, the best two-gas panel (C2H6 and C2H4) {{rg_best2_loss}}, and the best single gas (C2H4) {{rg_best1_loss}}.
- **Hydrogen alone**, the gas measured by the simplest monitors, gave a macro-F1 of only {{rg_h2only_f1}}, a loss of {{rg_h2only_loss}}.

That methane is the least necessary gas agrees with the explanation of the model in Chapter 5, where methane had the smallest permutation importance on the internal and Li test sets, and with the strong correlation of methane with the other hydrocarbons (@sec:describe): much of its information is carried by C2H6 and C2H4.

### Conventional Methods with Fewer Gases

The conventional methods depend on particular gases. The ratio methods need all five gases, and the Duval Triangle needs CH4, C2H4 and C2H2; running the MATLAB implementations with removed gases confirmed that each method gives no diagnosis for every record when a gas it needs is missing. @fig:upset shows the consequence: of the 31 gas combinations, IEC 60599 and Rogers can be applied to one and the Duval Triangle to four.

![Gas-subset matrix: the bars give the cross-validated macro-F1 (95 % interval) of every gas combination from best to worst, the dots show the gases included, and the bottom rows show which conventional methods can still be applied](12_Thesis/figures/thesis/subset_matrix.png){#fig:upset width=15.5}

The minimum panel therefore has a practical consequence: **no conventional method can interpret it**, because all of them need methane. The ML model keeps almost its full performance on a panel that the conventional methods cannot use. Conversely, the three gases of the Duval Triangle (CH4, C2H4 and C2H2) gave the ML model a macro-F1 of only {{rg_duvgases_f1}}.

### Implications for Online Monitoring

Online monitors that measure only hydrogen, or hydrogen and carbon monoxide, can detect that a fault is developing but cannot identify it (Bustamante *et al.*, 2019); the very low macro-F1 of hydrogen alone confirms this for the ML model as well. A monitor that measures H2, C2H6, C2H4 and C2H2 would, according to these results, support an ML diagnosis almost as good as a full laboratory analysis of the five gases. These results come from cross-validation on the development set and should be confirmed on independent data before they guide the choice of monitors.

## Exploratory Study: Can the Model Be Improved? {#sec:g-study2}

### Purpose

The main results showed that the released model did not beat the best conventional methods on the IEC TC 10 benchmark. The exploratory study examined three possible improvements under a protocol registered in advance (@sec:ch3): retraining on the pooled data sources (model B), adding the conventional diagnoses as inputs (hybrid model C) and a reject option. Because the test sources had already been seen in the main study, these results are exploratory and cannot change the main conclusions.

### Pooling and the Hybrid Model

@tab:study2 and @fig:s2f1 show the leave-one-source-out results.

[[TABLE:study2]] {#tab:study2}

![Macro-F1 with 95 % intervals of the three models and the three conventional methods when each source is held out in turn](12_Thesis/figures/thesis/study2_f1.png){#fig:s2f1 width=15.5}

Retraining on the pooled sources (B) and the hybrid model (C) gave higher point estimates than the released model (A) on every external source. On the IEC TC 10 power transformers the hybrid model reached a macro-F1 of {{s2_IECTC10_C_f1}} against {{s2_IECTC10_A_f1}} for the released model. However, neither of the rules fixed in the protocol was met. The improvement of B over A had a confidence interval that included zero on all three external sources, and C was not significantly better than B on any source; on the Dissanayake training source it was worse. Pooling and the hybrid design are therefore **not shown** to improve the model.

### The Reject Option

A conventional ratio method gives no diagnosis for part of the records and is usually right on the rest. The reject option lets the ML model behave in the same way: it answers only the records on which it is most confident. @tab:reject and @fig:s2rc compare the hybrid model with the ratio methods at the same coverage.

[[TABLE:study2_reject]] {#tab:reject}

![Accuracy on the answered records against coverage when the models abstain on their least confident records (lines), with the conventional methods as points](12_Thesis/figures/thesis/study2_coverage.png){#fig:s2rc width=15.5}

When the hybrid model abstained until its coverage equalled that of IEC 60599, it was more accurate on the records it answered than IEC 60599 on the records IEC 60599 answered, on every source. The confidence interval of the difference excluded zero on all sources except the IEC TC 10 power transformers, where the model reached {{s2r_IECTC10_IEC60599_sel}} against {{s2r_IECTC10_IEC60599_cond}} (difference interval {{s2r_IECTC10_IEC60599_ci}}). The curves in @fig:s2rc lie above the points of the ratio methods on every source: at the same coverage the model is more accurate.

## Discussion

The reduced-gas results fill a gap noted in the literature review: earlier studies imputed missing gas values (Wang *et al.*, 2023) or used monitor data with a fixed gas set (Jin *et al.*, 2024), but none measured, with a tolerance fixed in advance and confidence intervals, how much an ML model loses for every possible subset of the five gases. The result that methane is the least necessary gas, and that the minimum panel cannot be interpreted by any conventional method, is a concrete argument for ML in settings with reduced gas measurements.

The exploratory study suggests a practical way of using ML next to the conventional methods: the model answers when it is confident and refers the remaining samples to an expert, just as a ratio method gives no diagnosis. This "confident or refer" mode is safer than forcing an answer on every sample. It is consistent with the call for uncertainty-aware diagnosis in prognostics and health management (Kashiwagi *et al.*, 2025). Confirming it needs fresh inspection-confirmed data; a search of legitimate public sources for such data was carried out but found none that was not already used.

## Summary

Of the 31 gas subsets, only one reduced panel, H2 with C2H6, C2H4 and C2H2 (without methane), kept the loss of macro-F1 within the tolerance of 0.05; no conventional method can interpret this panel. Hydrogen alone was far from sufficient for diagnosis. In the exploratory study, pooling the sources and adding the conventional diagnoses as inputs did not significantly improve the model, but a reject option made the hybrid model more accurate than the ratio methods at the same coverage on every source.
